# Honey Cake Bakery — Birchington-on-Sea, Kent

Research notes for this prospect, run 2026-10-05.

## History check

the three most recent in full:

3. Hair, There & Everywhere (Haddington, 2026-09-21) — centrepiece was "The
   Whole Salon, In One Bag", a handful of named discrete props (cape,
   mirror, scissors, kit bag) flying in from off-screen and settling into a
   static room scene. Accent: wine/burgundy. Type: Frank Ruhl Libre +
   Manrope.
4. Bespoke Home & Garden Solutions (Mold, 2026-09-28) — centrepiece was
   "From Plan to Planted", one fixed set of shapes transitioning from thin
   ink outline to full coloured "built" form in place, with small elements
   (fence posts, stepping stones) growing at their own already-drawn
   positions. Accent: blueprint blue. Type: Newsreader + Space Grotesk.

So four different mechanics already used: (1) one continuous line/shape
interpolating, (2) a mask revealing a fixed illustration, (3) a few named
discrete props flying in from off-screen and assembling into a static
tableau, (4) one fixed set of shapes transitioning from outline to built
form in place. This run deliberately uses a fifth, different kind of
mechanic: **a swarm of roughly sixty small, uniform, unnamed particles
(hexagonal "flecks"), scattered randomly across the whole scene, that
individually converge from scattered positions into a precise honeycomb
lattice pattern forming the body of a cake** — not a single continuous
shape, not a mask, not a handful of named props, and not shapes growing in
place at their own already-drawn spot without travelling. See "The
centrepiece" below for the full breakdown.

It's also a different trade (bespoke cake maker, not pet care, a chimney
trade, hairdressing, or landscaping), a different accent hue family (honey
amber/gold, not sage green, ember red-orange, wine/burgundy, or blueprint
blue), a different type pairing (Spectral + Sora, neither used before, and
not Inter), and a different concrete spacing/radius scale (radius steps
14/30/60, deliberately the softest and most rounded of any run so far, to
fit a soft, iced, handmade feel rather than the sharp drafting-table feel
of the gardener run).

## The essence (Step 2)

"Bakery" undersells this one specifically because it has no shop window,
no display case, and no walk-in trade at all: every single source
describing it repeats the same phrase, "Private & Advance Bookings only
please." That is not incidental listing text, it is the defining fact of
how this business runs. Most bakeries bake first and sell whatever is on
the counter that day. This one only makes a cake once someone has actually
booked and described what it's for.

Layered on that: the business has a genuinely rare trust signal, a perfect
recommend rate on Facebook (100%, 37–38 reviews) AND a perfect rating on
Google (5.00/5, 32 reviews), two completely independent audiences agreeing,
on top of an officially verified five-star food hygiene rating from the
council. That is three separate, independently-sourced signals all
pointing the same way, for what reads as a small, likely one-person
operation. Put together, the essence is: **nothing here is made until you
ask for it, and once you do, it is made specifically for that one booking,
not pulled off a shelf; the near-unanimous praise across every platform
that rates it is the proof that this actually works.** That is what should
carry the design, not "cake shop" as a category, and not generic bakery
photography of finished cakes sitting in a display case, since this
business deliberately has none.

- Whimsical hand-drawn or watercolour-style branding.
- Dense portfolio grids organised by cake type or occasion.
- Dietary-option badges (vegan, gluten-free) displayed prominently.
- Soft pastel palettes, or a single elegant colour like navy for contrast.

**Kept:** a clear hero with a real phone CTA, a genuine services/occasions
section, a trust section built from publicly verifiable numbers rather
than invented quotes, a closing CTA repeating the real phone number, a
footer with the real address and hours.

**Deliberately broken:** no photography-led hero or portfolio gallery,
since no real photography of this business's actual cakes exists, and

with.

## Design decisions

- **Accent colour:** a honey amber/gold (`#bf7a12`, darker `#8a570c` for
  the trust section and hover states), chosen because it is literally what
  the business is named after, and reads as warm, confident and precise
  rather than the beige+brass "artisan" cliché or AI-purple. It's a
  different hue family entirely from all four previous runs (sage green,
  ember red-orange, wine/burgundy, blueprint blue) — this is a true
  yellow-gold rather than any of those.
- **Type pairing:** Spectral (a literary serif with real italics) for
  headings, Sora (a warm, geometric sans) for body copy. Neither font was
  used in any previous run (Fraunces + Work Sans, Bricolage Grotesque +
  Karla, Frank Ruhl Libre + Manrope, Newsreader + Space Grotesk), and
  neither is Inter.
- **Spacing/radius system:** an 8px-rooted spacing scale (`--space-1` to
  `--space-8`: 8, 16, 24, 32, 52, 80, 128, 192px), a different concrete set
  from every previous run's scale, and a three-step radius system
  (14px small elements, 30px cards, 60px large shapes) deliberately the
  softest and most rounded of any run so far, a conscious choice to feel
  like icing and soft bakes rather than the sharp drafting-table feel used
  for the landscaping run.

## The centrepiece (Step 4's "genuinely inventive moment")

**"The Gather."** A pinned, scroll-scrubbed scene built with GSAP +
ScrollTrigger, set against a dark backdrop. On load, roughly sixty small
hexagonal "flecks" (hand-generated with a hexagonal-packing algorithm, in
three honey tones) are displaced via `gsap.set` away from their authored,
final positions to random scattered points and rotations across the whole
scene, like sugar or sprinkles thrown in the air, with nothing yet in any
recognisable shape. As the visitor scrolls:

1. The ~36 flecks belonging to the bottom tier converge first, each
   travelling its own distance and rotation back to its precise final
   position, staggered randomly so they don't arrive as a single uniform
   block, assembling into a packed honeycomb lattice that fills a
   rounded-rectangle tier silhouette. Bottom tier first, matching how a
   real tiered cake is actually built, base up.
2. The ~24 flecks belonging to the top tier converge the same way shortly
   after, slightly overlapping the tail of the first wave.
3. The two tier outlines draw themselves in with a crisp stroke
   (`stroke-dashoffset`) to frame the now-assembled honeycomb mass.
4. A honey drip trail draws down the side of the top tier, with three
   small drip blobs popping into place.
5. A ribbon band and bow scale onto the bottom tier.
6. A candle fades in and settles upright, a soft warm glow blooms behind
   the whole cake, and the candle flame begins a slow, continuous flicker
   loop (motivated: real candle flames flicker, this isn't decoration for
   its own sake).

Four captions ("01 Nothing on the shelf, nothing pre-made", "02 Every fleck
placed on purpose", "03 Built around what you actually asked for", "04 One
cake. Booked ahead. Made once, for you.") crossfade in time with the same
scroll progress.

This is deliberately a different *kind* of moment from all four previous
runs: nothing is a single continuously-interpolating line or shape (unlike
the pulse line), there is no clip-path mask sliding over one fixed
illustration (unlike the soot reveal), it isn't a handful of named,
distinct props flying in from off-screen (unlike the kit-bag unpacking,
which used maybe eight identifiable objects), and nothing is simply
growing in place at a position it was always drawn at without travelling
(unlike the garden plan, where fence posts and stepping stones scale up
exactly where they already sit). Here it's a genuine particle swarm: about
sixty near-identical, unnamed elements, each individually travelling a
different distance from a randomised starting point to converge into one
specific, final, considered pattern. That is a literal visualisation of
the essence: nothing exists in any fixed form until a specific order pulls
it together into one exact result.

## Mobile and reduced-motion handling

- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is checked before anything else. If set,
  the page adds a `no-motion` class and returns immediately without
  building any GSAP timeline. Since every fleck, outline, drip, ribbon,
  candle and the glow are authored directly in the SVG at their *finished*
  state (packed honeycomb tiers, drawn outlines, visible drip, visible
  ribbon and bow, lit candle), the page already looks correct and complete
  with zero motion, no displacement ever happens without JS running.
- A second, independent fallback checks `typeof window.gsap === 'undefined'`
  and `typeof window.ScrollTrigger === 'undefined'`. If the CDN script
  fails to load or execute at all, the page adds a `no-gsap` class with the
  same "show everything, fully assembled" fallback, rather than leaving any
  `.reveal` element stuck at `opacity:0`.
- On viewports at or below 760px, `ScrollTrigger.matchMedia` swaps the
  centrepiece from a pinned scrub (`pin: '.cp-sticky'`, `end: '+=360%'`) to
  an unpinned one (`pin: false`, `end: 'bottom bottom'`), so the same
  timeline still plays as the visitor scrolls naturally past the section
  instead of hijacking touch scroll.

## Testing

cdnjs.cloudflare.com and Google Fonts are blocked in this sandbox for both
`curl` and headless-browser traffic, same as every previous run, so the
real GSAP/ScrollTrigger animation and real webfonts could not be loaded
against the actual CDN in this environment. To still catch real bugs
before shipping, I used Playwright with the sandbox's pre-installed
Chromium:

1. Rendered the real page with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` emulated
   (a real, unstubbed code path) at 1300px and 390px wide, scrolled
   through the whole page, and screenshotted it. **This caught one genuine
   bug**: the centrepiece's `.cp-svg-wrap` had a `max-height:62vh` that
   didn't match the box's actual rendered size (the SVG was sized by width
   via `height:auto`, so it overflowed the capped box without being
   clipped), and the overflowing cake artwork visually bled down into the
   caption row below it, leaving caption text overlapping the cake image.
   Fixed by giving the wrapper an explicit `aspect-ratio`, a tighter
   `max-height`, and `overflow:hidden` so the box and its content always
   agree and nothing bleeds into the next element.
2. While fixing the above, also noticed the warm background glow behind
   the candle was almost invisible, because the glow circle was drawn
   *after* the hex tiers in the SVG paint order, meaning it painted a thin
   translucent wash over the already-coloured honeycomb fill rather than
   behind it, which its chosen opacity was too subtle to show either way.
   Moved the glow circle to be the very first element painted in the SVG
   (true backdrop, behind the stand, tiers and everything else), enlarged
   it, and raised its final opacity, confirmed with a cropped, zoomed
   screenshot that a visible warm halo now surrounds the cake silhouette
   against the dark backdrop.
3. Served a small local stub of the `gsap`/`ScrollTrigger` API (`.set`,
   `.to`, `.timeline().to()`, `.utils.toArray`, `ScrollTrigger.matchMedia`)
   via Playwright request interception in place of the real CDN files,
   with the stub throwing on any null element or unmatched selector. The
   real, unmodified script ran against it without throwing at either
   1300px or 390px, confirming every element ID referenced in the script
   (all sixty fleck IDs, both tier outlines, the drip, drops, ribbon, bow,
   candle stick and flame) genuinely exists in the page.
4. Rendered the real page with the GSAP `<script>` tags blocked entirely
   (`route.abort()` on every script request), confirming the `no-gsap`
   fallback shows the full, fully-assembled page with no console errors
   and nothing stuck invisible.
5. Checked for horizontal overflow (`scrollWidth > clientWidth`) at both
   1300px and 390px in every scenario above: none found, in either the
   pre-fix or post-fix runs (the caption-overlap bug was a vertical/paint
   issue, not a horizontal overflow one, confirmed separately by visual
   screenshot review).
